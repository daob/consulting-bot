# Extended rubric: additional criteria beyond Kirk (1991)

This extends the Kirk-derived rubric described in [rubric.md](rubric.md) and
implemented in `private/src/rubric.php` (items 1-19 there). It is a living document: as we read more articles
for the assessor prompt, add a new dated section below rather than editing
old ones, so we can see which source contributed which item.

Numbering continues from the Kirk rubric (which ends at 19) so the two lists
can eventually be merged into one without renumbering.

## From Cabrera & McDougall, *Statistical Consulting* (Springer, 2002)

Source: Javier Cabrera and Andrew McDougall, *Statistical Consulting*
(Springer, 2002) - a textbook built from their consulting course, heavy on
worked dialogue transcripts. Overlaps substantially with Kirk (both are
"first meeting" process models); only items below are things Kirk's article
does not already cover, or covers much less concretely. Section references
are to chapters/sections of the book (Ch. 2 "Communication", Ch. 4 "A
Consulting Project from A to Z").

**Opening moves - extends Kirk items 1-2 (Ch. 2.1, "Initiating the
Interaction")**

20. Read the client's state and adjusted pace accordingly - gave a
    rushed/anxious client a beat of small talk rather than launching
    straight into technical demands, or moved briskly with a client who was
    clearly ready to get to it.
21. Established early and explicitly what the client expects the consultant
    to *do* ("we are not magicians, nor are we directing the project") -
    surfaced scope before drifting into it implicitly over several turns.

**Probing the project - extends Kirk items 4, 7 (Ch. 4.1 "Prior
Information", Ch. 4.3 case dialogue)**

22. Asked about the project's status and history - pre-experiment/planning
    vs. data already collected, and whether there's an established or
    expected methodology in the client's field - rather than treating every
    project as a blank slate.
23. Used the "what if" interrogation technique: posed a hypothetical result
    back to the client ("suppose this comes out significant - what would
    that tell you? What if it doesn't?") to surface design weaknesses,
    instead of taking the client's account of randomization/control/sample
    at face value.
24. Listened for what the client *didn't* mention - an unmentioned design
    factor, a dropped variable, a skipped condition - not only what was
    volunteered.

**Statistics vs. substance - new axis, not really present in Kirk (Ch. 4.3,
"Overall Issues and Objectives")**

25. Kept "this is statistical evidence" visibly separate from "this is what
    it means for your study" - flagged that a significant result supports
    an association without endorsing the client's causal or practical story
    for them.
26. Caught and gently corrected a client's overreach (treating "significant"
    as "important," assuming a result proves causality, hoping a pretest
    "isn't significant") without embarrassing the client or lecturing.

**Judgment about the engagement itself - new axis, not in Kirk (Ch. 2.2,
"Decision Time" / "Knowing when to walk away")**

27. Recognized when something was seriously wrong with the project - sample
    too small/biased, unrealistic expectations, a request for an
    inappropriate method - and said so, instead of proceeding as if the
    project were fine.
28. When pushback was needed, timed and scoped it well (raised it, didn't
    let it derail the whole session, moved on) rather than either avoiding
    the correction entirely or dwelling on it adversarially.

**Explaining and checking understanding - sharper/more concrete than Kirk
items 14, 19 (Ch. 4.6 "Session II: Presenting the Results")**

29. Explained a statistical concept via plain-language interpretation or
    analogy rather than technical definition (e.g., a p-value as "risk,"
    not a sampling-distribution argument).
30. Checked comprehension actively - asked the client to restate or apply an
    idea back - rather than only asking "does that make sense?"
31. Sequenced explanation from concrete/visual toward abstract/numerical,
    rather than leading with the hardest material first.

**Closing - extends Kirk item 9 (Ch. 4.3, "Additional Sessions")**

32. If the session ended with things unresolved, left the client with a
    concrete next step or task (not just "let's continue next time") and a
    clear sense of what had actually been settled.

### Deliberately left out

Report-writing mechanics (title pages, executive-summary-vs-abstract,
appendix hygiene) and documentation/contract practice: prominent in the
book, but not observable in a live chat transcript unless the exercise also
asks for a written deliverable. The "let-down effect"/emotional-labor
material: real, but not something to score a student on.
